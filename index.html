Under the hood

How a request flows

One server, one resident model, four checkpoint-specific backends. Generation is single-slot; everything else is served concurrently so a long prefill stays watchable.

Computepool.c (NEON threads) or metal.c (zero-copy arena)

C11 plus three Objective-C files — two Metal bridges (metal_objc.m, q35_gpu.m) and a QR encoder. It links only macOS system frameworks — Accelerate, Metal, CoreImage, ImageIO, CoreGraphics — and the system sqlite3. No Python, no BLAS, no runtime conversion step. One binary, four subcommands: setup, run, serve, doctor.

No endpoint matches that filter.

Generation is single-slot and requests queue in arrival order. Metrics, the model library, the log stream and the console stay answerable while a long prefill runs — that is what makes watching it possible at all.

Checkpoints

Four models, each with its own backend

Not a closed list by design — but every checkpoint needs its own loader, quantizer, prompt renderer and kernels. Decode figures are measured on an M3 Max with 128 GiB and the Metal backend.

ModelOn diskShape ContextDecode
GLM-5.3-Flash≈320B parameters · the original model ≈306 GiB62 shards 45 layers · 288 experts · top-8 · FP8-E4M3 500K 3.6 tok/s resident
DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017largest one that still fits a laptop ≈475 GiB48 shards 40 layers · 384 experts · top-6 · MLA + FP4 500K 2.8 tok/s
MiniMax-M3.1-Flashsmallest footprint of the three MoEs ≈233 GiB48 shards 60 layers (3 dense) · 128 experts · top-4 · NVFP4 500K 1.48 tok/s
DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Qwen3.5-9B-UNCENSORED-hereticdense, held wholly in RAM ≈18 GiB4 shards 32 layers: 24 Gated DeltaNet + 8 GQA · dense BF16 500K 20.4 tok/s on the GPU

Context is not free: at 500K positions the KV cache, the resident spine and the expert bank all draw from the same 80% RAM ceiling. Every figure on this page is measured, not assumed.
